Standing On

Not people, but worth naming: Hertzforge is written against a body of work it does not include and could not replace. Papers are cited by author, title and venue. The control laws and the reference tools of MIMO Random and MIMO Shock were written from these papers, and their code comments cite them the same way.

  • Bendat and Piersol, Random Data: Analysis and Measurement Procedures. The spectral conventions follow it. Where the controller holds the complex conjugate of its cross-spectral definition, as the multiple-shaker control literature does, the documentation says so at every boundary.
  • Visual Dynamics, Brandon Zwink's structural dynamics toolset, from which Hertzforge's analysis core comes. Its own acknowledgments carry over with it.
  • The UFF dataset 58 convention, for a fifty-year-old format that still says what a channel is and what it measures.
  • P. D. Welch, "The Use of Fast Fourier Transform for the Estimation of Power Spectra", IEEE Transactions on Audio and Electroacoustics, 1967: the averaged spectral estimates every loop is built on.
  • IEC 60068-2-80, Environmental testing, Part 2-80: Tests, Test Fi: Vibration, Mixed mode: the test type Mixed Mode controls, a random floor with sine tones or narrow bands of random laid over it. The control method is Hertzforge's own composition of published building blocks, each held to a closed-form case in its tests.
  • S. O. Rice, "Mathematical Analysis of Random Noise", Bell System Technical Journal, 1944 and 1945: the statistics of a sine wave in random noise, behind how far a tone must stand over the floor to be read and the predicted peak of the two together.
  • F. J. Harris, "On the Use of Windows for Harmonic Analysis with the Discrete Fourier Transform", Proceedings of the IEEE, 1978: how far a tone leaks into the lines beside it, which sets the width Mixed Mode leaves out of the floor's average around each tone.
  • D. O. Smallwood, the multiple-shaker random control papers: "Multiple Shaker Random Vibration Control: An Update", Proceedings of the IEST, 1999, the source of the default law's closed-loop correction of a factor of the reference and its per-loop limits; and "Random Vibration Testing of a Single Test Item with a Multiple Input Control System", Proceedings of the IES, 1982, where the lineage starts; and "Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (MIMO) Linear Systems Extreme Inputs/Outputs", Shock and Vibration, 2007, with "Minimum Input Trace for Multiple Input Multiple Output Linear Systems", Journal of the IEST, 2013: the minimum drive principle, the exact-control end of the minimum-drive law.
  • K. Coletti, R. B. Davis and R. Schultz, "Pareto-optimal target definition for multi-axis random vibration testing", 2026: the minimum-drive law's problem, the drive power traded against the autospectrum residuals over the positive semi-definite cone, and the automatic choice of the trade-off at the knee of the curve.
  • D. O. Smallwood and T. L. Paez, "A Frequency Domain Method for the Generation of Partially Coherent Normal Stationary Time Domain Signals", Shock and Vibration, 1993: drive synthesis from a drive spectral density matrix.
  • R. Schultz and P. Avitabile, "Shape-Constrained Input Estimation for Multi-Shaker Vibration Testing", IMAC proceedings, and "Shape-Constrained Input Estimation for Efficient Multi-shaker Vibration Testing", Experimental Techniques, 2020: the shape-constrained law.
  • R. Schultz, "A Demonstration of Force Estimation and Regularization Methods for Multi-Shaker Testing", IMAC proceedings: the regularization choices and the condition-number limit of the identification gate.
  • P. M. Daborn, C. Roberts, D. J. Ewins and P. R. Ind, "Next-Generation Random Vibration Tests", IMAC XXXII, 2014: the buzz-test method, matching the structure's own coherence and phase, as described by Schultz.
  • N. Hunter, K. Cross and G. Nelson, "The Cross Spectrum in Multiple Input Multiple Response Vibration Testing", IMAC proceedings: which references a signal can have, entering one as autospectra, coherence and phase, and the eigenvalue clamp that repairs one that cannot be had.
  • L. Jacobs, M. Ross, G. Tipton, G. Nelson, K. Cross, N. Hunter and J. Harvie, "6-DOF Shaker Test Input Derivation from Field Test" and "Experimental Execution of 6DOF Tests Derived from Field Tests", IMAC XXXV, 2017: six degree-of-freedom references from field data.
  • J. Paripovic and R. L. Mayes, "Reproducing a Component Field Environment on a Six Degree-of-Freedom Shaker", IMAC proceedings: six degree-of-freedom control in practice, identification with uncorrelated inputs, and the conditioning a working test can tolerate.
  • N. J. Higham, "Computing a Nearest Symmetric Positive Semidefinite Matrix", Linear Algebra and its Applications, 1988: the nearest realizable matrix, the mathematics under the reference repair.
  • Fisher, "Computer-Controlled Multiple-Actuator Shaker Offers Versatile Test Capabilities", report, 1977, OSTI 7328357, and Rader and Berry, "Experimental Investigation of Launch Vehicle Transient Input Simulation in Payload Tests", NASA CR-145349, 1979: open-loop transient control with several exciters, a measured system inverted to reproduce a waveform at several points at once, where MIMO Shock's classical method starts.
  • R. Schultz, "Explorations in Multiple-Input Shaker Shock Testing", 2019, and R. Schultz and S. Carter, "A MIMO Time Waveform Replication Control Implementation", 2022, both conference papers of Sandia National Laboratories: the regularized inverse of the identified system on each spectral line, the zero padding that keeps a pulse from wrapping around its record, and filtering the target into the band the identification can be trusted in.
  • Coleman, Smallwood and Skousen, "Response Limited Shaker Shock Testing", 2016, a conference paper of Sandia National Laboratories: the published one-exciter SRS correction, which updates only the magnitude of the estimated system from the ratio of the measured SRS to the target, leaves its phase alone, keeps the weight under one and rises in 3 dB steps. The default SRS law, shape, is its form for several exciters.
  • Owens, Hatonen and Daley, "Robust Gradient-Based Discrete-Time Iterative Learning Control Algorithms", University of Sheffield research report, 2006: the convergence condition of iterative learning control under model error, behind the convergence checks the laws are held to and the correction gain kept under one.
  • B. Gustavsen and A. Semlyen, "Rational Approximation of Frequency Domain Responses by Vector Fitting", IEEE Transactions on Power Delivery, 1999, with Gustavsen's relaxed form, and G. H. Golub and V. Pereyra, "The Differentiation of Pseudo-Inverses and Nonlinear Least Squares Problems Whose Variables Separate", SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis, 1973: the modal fit of the identified system that the laws' tracked model option starts from.
  • MIL-STD-810H, Method 516.8 (Shock) and Method 527.2 (Multi-Exciter Test), and IEC 60068-2-27, Environmental testing, Part 2-27: Tests, Test Ea and guidance: Shock: the classical pulses and their tolerances, the SRS tolerance and the effective duration window each control point is judged by, and the energy-weighted global SRS error MIMO Shock shows beside its verdict.
  • Rattlesnake, the open-source MIMO vibration controller by Sandia National Laboratories (its packaging names Daniel P. Rohe as author), for showing what an open MIMO controller should do. Credited as a source, not an affiliation. Nothing of its code is in Hertzforge: Rattlesnake is GPL-3.0 and Hertzforge is an MIT project, so its code could not have been taken. Its features, workflow and file layouts were read from its public documentation only, and the methods were written here from the published literature listed above.
  • sdynpy, for showing what a structural dynamics toolset in Python should do. Hertzforge reads its data files; nothing of its code is in Hertzforge, since it is GPL-3.0 and its code could not have been taken into an MIT project.
